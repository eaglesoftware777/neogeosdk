PROLOGUE — A VALLEY THAT STILL NEEDS HELP

Smoggar’s rule is over, but the valley has not recovered everything it lost. Machinery was stripped for parts. Roads were neglected. A storm has damaged fences and roofs. Now the irrigation channel beside Willowbrook’s bean field is dry again.

Maiya sees a problem and wants to act: dig a new channel, lift a stone, climb until she can see what is wrong. Her twin sister Luna wants to understand the cause before deciding what to repair. She reads small marks, studies mechanisms and keeps a notebook full of observations.

Their different instincts lead to the same decision. Someone must go upstream.

The player chooses who sets out. If Maiya travels, Luna lends her notebook and magnifying lens, then stays to mend storm damage with the villagers. If Luna travels, Maiya remains behind to help haul stones and repair fences.

The traveller goes to Elder Nari at the well. Nari explains that Willowbrook’s channels stopped flowing when the old watermill stopped. Master Tovan, who knows its machinery, has been waiting by the wheel.

An investigation into a dry field becomes the beginning of a journey through the valley’s forgotten engineering.

CHAPTER I — THE FORGOTTEN WATERMILL
VALLEY OF SACRED FALLS

Tovan shows the traveller an incomplete gear train. The timbers and axle are sound, but the bronze cog that connected the wheel to the millstone is missing. Without it, the mechanism cannot lift the irrigation sluice.

Smoggar’s crews carried the cog away and abandoned it in the Mossy Cave beyond the forest.

The route introduces the sisters’ different ways of solving problems. Maiya climbs ivy, moves heavy blocks and uses her whip to shoo obstructing creatures. Luna finds narrow clefts, reaches delicate catches and works mechanisms that require a steady hand.

In the cave, the old miners left instructions in musical chimes. Reading the marks and ringing the notes in order opens the way. A chasm can be crossed using the traveller’s particular strengths: a heavy block for Maiya, an old folding footbridge for Luna.

The bronze cog returns to the mill. But a repaired gear train alone is not enough. The water must follow the correct route: the high sluice opens, the spill closes, and the safety system can lift the floodgate.

The wheel turns. Water reaches Willowbrook. Hana’s beans stand upright again.

Behind the wheel, Sunboy finds a stone passage containing drawings of seven great structures. The mill is only the first wonder. The drawings suggest that these works belong to one connected system.

Director Valen, who once kept Smoggar’s works running, takes an interest. He imagines all seven machines managed by a single central office. The traveller asks who would decide which village receives water. Valen answers with the language of efficiency and order.

For now, six wonders remain silent.

CHAPTER II — THE EMERALD LABYRINTH
EMERALD FOREST

The botanist Mina needs help. Landslides and fallen trees have isolated the forest research station, along with its survey of the valley’s safe roads.

Without current information, village crews cannot know where to repair paths or send travellers. The forest has become a barrier between communities.

The old hedge labyrinth offers a way around the landslide. Its trail markers reward attention: the oak leaf on each marker points along the safe route. Piko’s interest in a hidden opening also leads the traveller toward a sheltered hollow.

Beyond the hedges, a pulley basket hangs over the misty ravine. Mina supplies a replacement rope. The traveller repairs the crossing, times the moving basket and reaches an old suspension bridge whose missing planks must be recovered.

Maiya searches the high places she can climb. Luna investigates gaps and passages suited to her smaller, more precise movements. In the bog garden, deer tracks reveal which ground will hold a person’s weight.

At the research station, the Forest Survey Map brings the scattered clues together. The semaphore mast sends its message across the valley. Road crews can finally clear the routes that the survey identifies as safe.

The valley is connected again. Valen admires the information system, but still imagines how it might be controlled from an office. The traveller is learning how much knowledge already lives with the people on the ground.

A message arrives from Captain Orin: the coastal lighthouse has gone dark.

CHAPTER III — THE LIGHTHOUSE OF SEVEN MIRRORS
AZURE CORAL COAST

At Coral Harbour, boats remain tied to the quay. Without the lighthouse, the reef makes the harbour entrance too dangerous after sunset.

Captain Orin directs the traveller to Keeper Marra. Smoggar’s crews disturbed the tower’s mechanisms and knocked its mirrors out of alignment. Marra knows what needs doing, but cannot do all the climbing and turning herself.

Four outdoor mirrors carry morning sunlight toward the heliograph latch on the tower door. Once their reflections reach the brass plate, the entrance opens.

Inside, the lamp’s clockwork is missing its escapement wheel. Marra has a spare in the supply cave, but the sea covers its approach except at low tide. Orin reads the tide table, giving the traveller a safe window to cross the exposed sandbar.

With the escapement restored, the clock turns the lamp and operates the gallery shutters. Three more mirrors send light toward the keepers’ sealed chamber. Together with the four outside, they give the lighthouse its seven mirrors.

The chamber protects a Precision Optical Lens made by the old builders. Returned to the lamp’s cradle, it brings the beam into focus.

That night, boats can find the harbour again.

A carved inscription expresses the builders’ purpose: light for sailors, water for farmers and a road for every foot. Their works were designed to make daily life possible across the whole valley.

Sunboy has found a map of an ancient orchard. The next part of the system lies inland.

CHAPTER IV — THE CLOCKWORK ORCHARD
GOLDEN AUTUMN GROVE

In Amberfield, Auntie Pell keeps the trees alive by carrying buckets. The clock tower that once drove the orchard’s machinery has lost its gears. Pumps and travelling platforms stand still.

Pell saved one gear when Smoggar’s crews arrived. The traveller searches for the others and studies the clockmaker’s rule: three gears touching in a closed ring cannot turn. Filling every socket would lock the mechanism. The correct pair restores motion.

The tower wakes, and its platforms carry the traveller across the irrigation ditch. An old harvester, repaired with oil, clears fallen branches from the terrace route.

Higher up, a counterbalanced lift responds to weight. A barrel and the traveller together provide what it needs. The ascent reaches the reservoir sluice, an Ancient Gear Assembly and part of the broken pipework.

The final repair joins the missing pipe sections so that water can reach every row.

The orchard drinks again. The trees have a future beyond another day of buckets.

Pell brings the next news: Dara, a cave surveyor, reports that the river beneath the crystal grotto has stopped.

CHAPTER V — THE CRYSTAL GROTTO ENIGMA
CRYSTAL GROTTO

Dara waits beside the cave entrance. Smoggar’s miners dammed the underground river to flood their old workings. The ventilation fan stopped, leaving the deep tunnels dangerous to enter.

The grotto looks mysterious, but its secrets can be understood. The builders cut crystal faces to reflect daylight. Aligning three of them sends light to the mechanism that opens the inner door.

Sound provides another set of clues. In the echo chamber, the cave returns a sequence in reverse, so the traveller reconstructs the original tune. At the whispering junction, a long, delayed echo identifies the open tunnel; short echoes reveal routes that turn back.

In the mine, the flood valve must close before the drowned passage can drain. Opening the race turns a waterwheel, which drives the ventilation fan and clears the air.

The surveyors’ hall becomes reachable. Its Underground Survey Instrument and records describe the river’s old course. The outflow sluice opens, and clear water finally leaves the grotto for the thirsty valley below.

A concealed crystal garden rewards further observation. Throughout the journey, the notebook gathers flowers, diagrams, photographs and traces of the people who built and used these places.

Five wonders now work again. The last bearings must be taken from the World Tree Observatory.

CHAPTER VI — THE WORLD TREE OBSERVATORY
SACRED WORLD TREE

At the roots of the enormous World Tree, the travelling sister hears a familiar voice. Her twin has finished helping Willowbrook and followed the reopened roads to catch up.

For this chapter, they travel together. They can take turns leading, wait on a mechanism or follow one another. Their different strengths become a shared resource.

A released brake allows the counterweighted cable car to reach the canopy. Among the branches, levers swing hanging bridges between platforms. Choosing one route can move another bridge out of reach, so progress depends on understanding the connections.

At the sun terrace, bronze mirrors carry light to three marked stones associated with dawn, noon and dusk. The marks operate mechanical catches; once all three are set, the stair opens.

Above them, astronomer Sefa explains that the builders used the stars to map the valley. The observatory has lost its dome gear and telescope lens.

Four calendar dials reveal another perspective on familiar knowledge. The tree-keepers began their year with falling leaves: autumn, winter, spring, summer. Setting that order releases the dome gear.

The Precision Optical Lens from the lighthouse also fits the telescope. The builders designed their instruments to share parts.

With the dome open, Sefa takes the bearings. All six wonders point toward the same hidden place: a great underground reservoir. He records its location in the Master Navigation Chart.

The answer leads back to the passage behind Willowbrook’s watermill. But Valen’s crews have already gone down with drills and pumps.

CHAPTER VII — THE GREAT VALLEY RESTORATION
THE HYDRAULIC HALLS

At the mill, the sisters divide their responsibilities. The sister who joined the journey at the World Tree remains above with Tovan, ready to operate the old spill if the water rises. The original traveller descends alone.

Below lies the purpose of the entire network: a distribution machine that shares the reservoir’s water between the valley’s regions.

Foreman Brack admits what Valen intends. A new channel will divert the whole reservoir to his central plant. Meanwhile, rising water has trapped workers in the lower bay. The people carrying out the plan are now threatened by it.

The machine wakes. Its records and instruments account for flow, roads, tides, crops, the underground river and the seasons.

A bridge collapses behind the traveller. Retreat is impossible, but the workers can open another exit once they are free.

The drill stops before it breaks through the reservoir wall.

The reservoir opens into the restored distribution system.

THE SEVENTH WONDER — THE BUILDERS’ CHARTER

A sealed charter explains the principle behind everything the traveller has repaired. Each region receives water according to season and need, measured through the six wonders.

The seventh wonder is the agreement that makes the machinery useful to everyone.

Valen’s central plan cannot account for the forest, coast, orchards and river as the people living there can. The traveller has seen what happens when any part is cut off.

Valen accepts that the old system already possessed an order he had failed to recognize. His crews will repair the damage under the villages’ council rather than above it. He will answer for the drilling.

The conflict ends through rescue, evidence and a working alternative. The valley’s future no longer depends on one office deciding what everybody else needs.
