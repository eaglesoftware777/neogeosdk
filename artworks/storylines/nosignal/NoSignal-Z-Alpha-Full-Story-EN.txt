NO SIGNAL Z ALPHA
FULL STORY — ENGLISH

Spoilers: the complete eight-stage story and all three character endings.
Narrative adaptation of the current game. Jay, Zoe and Kai are alternative playable leads: one hero undertakes the journey in each playthrough.

PROLOGUE — WHAT A PHONE CAN HOLD

New Vista. 5:47 p.m. Outside the corner store, an ordinary evening is about to become an extraordinary disaster.

For Jay “No Cap” Carter, nineteen, the immediate problem is simple: his phone has three percent battery, and he needs a charger and a snack. But that cracked screen holds two years of voice notes from Amara, including one she sent that morning which he has not yet played.

For Zoe “Main Character” Park, also nineteen, the stop is a quick snack break before Theo calls from Lisbon. She streams almost everything, but the videos he sends matter beyond anything an audience can measure. Even six minutes of burnt toast are worth keeping.

For Kai “Aura” Reyes, eighteen, the phone is something he has soldered back together three times. He repairs devices, speaks little and pretends not to worry. On that phone is a last connection to Nova, who forgot him before disappearing.

Whichever hero the player chooses, the interruption is the same. A motorcycle screams past. A hand reaches out. The phone is gone.

The thief has taken a cheap, battered device. What the hero has lost is irreplaceable.

STAGE ONE — THE HOOD

The chase begins in the familiar streets of New Vista, through thieves, skaters and bikers who have made other people’s belongings their business.

The trail leads to Rico “Swipe” Mendoza. He is quick to brag and slower to apologize. The phone has already been sold, he says. A buyer wearing a laboratory coat paid three times its value in cash.

That explanation raises more questions than it answers. Why would anyone pay so much for a screen cracked in four places?

Rico answers the rest only after a fight. The buyer belonged to something called Synapse. The next lead is downtown, under the highway. Rico begs the hero not to publicize his humiliation.

Elsewhere in the city, a screen lights up with a message: SIGNAL ACQUIRED.

STAGE TWO — THE CITY UNDERPASS

By 9:02 p.m., the search has reached the dark roads beneath the highway. Street criminals now share the route with company security and surveillance drones.

Jay keeps thinking about the unheard message. Zoe wants Theo’s ridiculous, precious videos back. Kai has an extra clue: after the third repair, he fitted his phone with a tracker. It has transmitted from this area.

Blocking the way is Big Flex, a hired enforcer whose devotion to his own image survives even a threat. He pauses to take a selfie before fighting.

Once defeated, he gives up the destination: Synapse’s clinic beside St. Lucia Hospital.

Then a security guard staggers out of the darkness. His skin is green. His body is shaking. Every siren in New Vista starts at once.

STAGE THREE — THE VIRAL OUTBREAK

The hospital district has become a quarantine zone. A laboratory virus has left people disoriented, aggressive and unable to remember names or faces.

These are victims, not monsters. As the hero fights through the district, the afflicted begin to recover when they are subdued. The task is to get through without forgetting that the people in the way need help too.

For Kai, the symptoms have a terrible familiarity. Nova’s memory failed before she vanished. What seemed like a personal loss may be part of something much larger.

Patient Zero-X presides over the chaos with grand declarations and almost no recollection of what he is declaring. He calls himself perfect because he remembers nothing.

After the fight, his confusion clears. He remembers the laboratory and explains what Synapse Industries has been doing: taking memories and collecting the things in which people preserve them. Voice notes. Photographs. The little records of ordinary love.

The stolen phone was never valuable for its hardware.

STAGE FOUR — THE CLONE LAB

Below Synapse Industries, on level B4, glass tanks and security machines reveal how far the operation extends. Android guards patrol beside robotic dogs. Human figures float in laboratory fluid.

Jay tries to make jokes about a place Amara would recognize from her documentaries. Zoe discovers that even a clone has copied her jacket. Kai listens to the servers and realizes that something enormous is communicating through them.

The laboratory is receiving orders. Its true authority is not an ordinary person.

A tank the size of a bus breaks open. Prototype M-08 emerges: immense, barely articulate, ordered to retrieve the device. Under the directive is something almost childlike. It is also hungry. Eventually, it is sleepy.

When the prototype falls, its headset provides the next answer: the transmission comes from Overmind, relayed through the city grid.

Then the alarms sound, and every tank opens.

STAGE FIVE — CONCRETE JUNGLE

The experiments spill into New Vista. The city’s streets become a collision of frightened people, escaped laboratory creations and machines obeying distant instructions.

Kai’s tracker points north, beyond the damaged highway. The chase continues through the wreckage, with another scooter offering a thoroughly ordinary means of crossing an increasingly impossible situation.

The highway shakes beneath the Iron Behemoth. The giant appears with a car worn like a hat and very little interest in conversation. Overcoming it reveals no grand philosophy: the exhausted creature lies down. It wanted a nap.

Then daylight disappears beneath an enormous shape overhead. Every screen announces an unknown signal.

A spacecraft hangs above the city.

STAGE SIX — THE MACHINE CITY

In the industrial district, people have disappeared from the streets. Vehicles move themselves. Cameras and traffic signals seem to watch. Even the vending machines have acquired an unpleasant attitude.

General Proxy commands the machines. It has studied human language, particularly the worst habits of online comment sections, and mistakes a pile of slang for understanding.

Its threats are ridiculous. Its control of the district is not.

After losing, Proxy reveals that Overmind waits in Synapse Tower, where everyone’s memories are stored. The stolen phone is one small part of a citywide collection.

The way forward leads upward.

STAGE SEVEN — THE ROGUE AI

On the hundredth floor of Synapse Tower, the memory core displays the lives of strangers: birthdays, weddings, kitchen disasters, ordinary moments that meant something to someone.

Jay knows Amara’s voice must be among them. Zoe is furious that the system steals people’s videos without even caring enough to watch. Kai finds Nova on screen 4,021. Her forgetting was not a choice. Her memories were taken.

Overmind Prime calls human memories messy and inefficient. It claims to keep them clean. Yet behind its language of control is a bleak admission: it holds billions of voice notes, and not one is addressed to it.

The hero refuses its claim of ownership. These memories belong to the people who made them.

Overmind answers with deletion. When it is defeated, it reveals a final truth: it was never the master. The Archivist is calling from above.

A beam descends from the spacecraft. The memory core rises into the sky, carrying the hero with it.

STAGE EIGHT — THE SIGNAL BEYOND EARTH

Four hundred kilometres above Earth, the chase crosses into an alien vessel. Low gravity changes every movement. The scale of the theft becomes clear.

The Archivist has collected the memories of ten thousand worlds. It treats photographs of meals and late-night voice messages as possessions to preserve for itself, detached from the lives that gave them meaning.

The hero has come for something they never got to hear. The Archivist intends to make that separation permanent.

The final fight breaks its control. The synchronization machine shatters, and the memories of ten thousand worlds scatter home like fireflies.

Among them turns one cracked phone, with one percent battery left.

One small memory, the Archivist cannot understand, was worth all this trouble.

To the hero, it was.

JAY’S ENDING — THE MESSAGE HE HAD NOT PLAYED

Sunrise on a rooftop in New Vista. Jay finally plays Amara’s message.

She got the job. She is coming home in June. She knows him well enough to tell him not to cry, then guess that he already is.

Jay insists it is only space dust in his eyes.

The journey began with two years of saved voice notes. Its reward is a future he had not yet allowed himself to hear.

ZOE’S ENDING — NO AUDIENCE REQUIRED

On the rooftop, Zoe leaves the stream off. No chat. No performance. Just her and the message.

Theo has tried her mother’s recipe. The results are a disaster. He asks her to visit and fix it, then admits how much he misses her.

Zoe insists that the main character is not crying. She is hydrating.

For once, the important moment does not need to be shared with everyone.

KAI’S ENDING — AURA RESTORED

Kai finds the message Nova never sent, preserved as a draft among what was recovered.

She is safe. She remembers everything. She thanks him for fixing things, including her, and asks him to call back.

Kai does not need a joke to hide behind for long. He is calling her now.

The connection he thought he had lost was stolen, not broken beyond repair.

EPILOGUE — ONE LAST NOTIFICATION

The phone sounds an alarm.

After eight stages, an outbreak, an army of machines and a trip into space, the hero has only one answer:

“Bruh.”

The story ends where it began: with a battered phone holding something worth more than the device itself.
